All are real recordings, not synthesised. For laughs, applause and crowds, see the Laughs & Applause category; for screams, sighs and burps, see Human & Voice Reactions.

Place the sound right on the funny frame and keep it short: a boing on a jump, a rimshot or air horn on a punchline, a record scratch on a freeze frame, crickets when a joke falls flat. Do not overuse them; one or two per joke is enough.

Funny sound effects work because of timing, not volume. Put a boing exactly on the frame where someone jumps or a head pops into shot, an air horn right on the punchline, and a twang when something lands awkwardly or a joke falls flat. Use them sparingly so each one still gets a laugh. A rimshot after a pun, a record scratch on a freeze frame, crickets after silence, a wrong-answer buzzer on a mistake and a frying-pan bonk on a slapstick hit are the meme sounds editors reach for most. These cartoon sound effects are popular in meme edits, reaction videos, kids' content and comedy shorts.
