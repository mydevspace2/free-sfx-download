About these whoosh & transition sound effects

Whooshes are the most used sound effects in short-form video. A quick whoosh under a cut, a zoom or a sliding title tells the viewer that something moved, even when the picture change is subtle. Every whoosh in this pack is a real recording: ropes, bamboo, wooden sheets, metal tubes and blades swung through the air past a microphone, plus a few short game-style swishes. They are trimmed so the sound starts on the first frame, have short fades so they never click, and are levelled so you can drop them on the timeline without hunting for the volume slider.

Tip: place the loudest part of the whoosh one or two frames before the cut, not exactly on it. The ear expects the movement to lead the picture. Short swipes (under 0.4 s) suit fast text pops and memes; longer rope and tube whooshes suit slow zooms and scene changes.

These transition sound effects for video editing cover every common move: a fast whoosh for a jump cut, a swipe for a slide transition, a deep tube whoosh for a cinematic zoom, and soft sheet and rope whooshes for calm vlogs where a hard swoosh would feel too loud. Because each whoosh sound effect has a different length and pitch, you can pick two or three and rotate them through a long edit so repeated transitions never sound copied. They work the same in CapCut, VN, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut Pro.
