How to use free sound effects in your videos

Sound effects (SFX) are the quickest way to make an edit feel professional. Viewers often do not notice a good whoosh or a soft click, but they immediately feel when a transition, a pop-up title or a zoom happens in silence. This page gives you a compact, carefully cleaned library of 151 free sound effects and a short guide to using them well, whether you edit in CapCut on your phone, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or any other editor.

Every file here is released under CC0 (public domain). That means you can use the sounds in monetised YouTube videos, TikToks, Instagram Reels, Facebook ads, podcasts, games, apps and client projects without paying, without attribution and without asking anyone. The full list of original sources is on our License page.

What makes these sound effects “editor-ready”?

Many free sound packs are hard to use: the sound starts half a second late, the volume is all over the place, or the file clicks when it ends. Each sound on this site has gone through the same four steps:

  1. Trimmed – leading silence is removed so the sound hits on the first frame you place it.
  2. Faded – a few milliseconds of fade-in and a short fade-out stop clicks and pops at the edges.
  3. Levelled – loudness is brought towards about -14 LUFS (the level most streaming platforms use), so sounds sit at a similar volume.
  4. Peak-safe – true peaks are capped at -1 dBTP so nothing distorts after export or platform compression.

Five timing tips that make SFX sound professional

  1. Lead the cut slightly. Place whooshes so their loudest point lands one or two frames before the visual change. Our ears expect movement to arrive just before the picture.
  2. Layer, don’t stack. A short whoosh followed by an impact on the next frame sounds bigger than one loud sound. Two quiet layers beat one loud layer.
  3. Keep SFX under the voice. Dialogue should stay on top. Pull sound effects 3–8 dB below your voice track, and lower the music when a key sound plays.
  4. Vary repeated sounds. When the same action repeats (subtitle pops, clicks, typing), alternate between two or three similar files from the same category so it does not sound robotic.
  5. Silence is a sound too. Dropping the music for half a second right before a big impact or boom makes the hit feel twice as strong.

How to add a downloaded sound in popular editors

CapCut (mobile): tap Audio → Extracted / Your sounds → Import, choose the MP3 from your Downloads folder and drag it under the clip. Premiere Pro: drag the WAV or MP3 into the Project panel, then onto an audio track under the cut. DaVinci Resolve: import into the Media Pool, then drop it on the timeline; use the clip volume handle to fine-tune. Final Cut Pro: drag the file into the browser and connect it to the clip at the playhead. In every editor you can trim the end of a sound or shorten it with a fade if you only need the first part.

Frequently asked questions

Are these sound effects really free for commercial use?

Yes. Every sound on this site comes from a CC0 (public domain dedication) source: BigSoundBank by Joseph Sardin, Kenney.nl and OpenGameArt CC0 uploads. CC0 means you can use them in monetised YouTube videos, TikToks, Reels, ads, games, apps and client work without paying and without asking permission.

Do I need to give credit?

No credit is required under CC0. A credit such as "Sound effects: BigSoundBank.com, Kenney.nl" is appreciated by the original creators but optional. The full source list is on the License page.

Will I get a YouTube copyright claim?

BigSoundBank states that its sounds are not registered in Content ID, and the Kenney and OpenGameArt sounds are public domain. Very rarely, a third party wrongly registers a video that contains a public domain sound. If that happens, dispute the claim and point to the original CC0 source listed on our License page.

Should I download MP3 or WAV?

MP3 (320 kbps) is small and works everywhere, including CapCut and phone editors. WAV (48 kHz, 24-bit) is uncompressed and best for Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut when you plan to process the sound further. Both versions are identical edits.

Can I listen before downloading?

Yes. Every sound card has a play button, so you can preview the sound in your browser and then download the MP3 or WAV in one click. Only one preview plays at a time, and pressing the button again stops it.

How loud are the files?

Each sound is trimmed (no silence at the start), given short fades so it never clicks, and levelled towards about -14 LUFS with true peaks kept at -1 dBTP. Very short transients such as clicks and punches stay peak-limited, so they sound punchy without distortion.

Can I download a whole category at once?

Yes. Every category page has a "Download whole category as ZIP" button. The ZIP contains both MP3 and WAV versions plus a licence text file.

Can I re-upload these sounds to my own site or sell them?

CC0 allows it legally, but we kindly ask you to link to this site instead of re-hosting the packs. Selling them as-is is not a fair use of free public-domain work.
